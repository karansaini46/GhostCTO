# Onboarding Emails

## Email 1: Welcome

Subject: Welcome to GhostCTO

Preview: Start by making the first build hard to misunderstand.

Body:

Welcome to GhostCTO.

The goal is simple: before you hire a developer, get clear on what to build, what stack to use, what to ask, what it should cost, and what red flags to avoid.

Start with one project. Add the founder context first:

- Who the product is for
- What problem it solves
- The must-have features
- Your budget range
- Your launch timeline
- Your biggest concern before hiring

Once that context is in place, generate the first scope and stack materials. Those documents become the base for hiring questions, quote review, and technical decisions.

CTA: Create your first project

## Email 2: Start With Scope

Subject: Start with scope, not stack

Preview: The first risk is usually an unclear brief.

Body:

A developer cannot estimate what is still vague.

Before you ask for a quote, use GhostCTO to define the first build:

- Core user roles
- Must-have workflows
- Explicit exclusions
- Acceptance criteria
- Milestones a developer can estimate

The most useful scope is not the biggest version of the idea. It is the smallest version that can be built, tested, and explained without constant interpretation.

CTA: Generate your scope brief

## Email 3: Use Stack Advice To Control Tradeoffs

Subject: Make the stack decision easier to challenge

Preview: You do not need to become technical. You do need better questions.

Body:

Founders often get stack recommendations that sound confident but do not match the stage.

GhostCTO helps you compare technical choices against budget, speed, hiring availability, maintenance, and future flexibility.

Use the stack guidance to ask:

- Why this stack for this launch?
- What does it make easier?
- What does it make harder?
- Will another developer be able to maintain it?
- What costs or vendor dependencies should I understand?

You stay in control of the decision, even when a developer owns the implementation.

CTA: Review stack guidance

## Email 4: Prepare For Developer Calls

Subject: Take better questions into developer calls

Preview: Strong answers are easier to spot when you know what to listen for.

Body:

A good developer call should make the project clearer.

Use GhostCTO to prepare a hiring packet with:

- Role brief
- Screening questions
- Strong-answer signals
- Practical work sample
- Evaluation rubric
- Project-specific red flags

You are not trying to out-engineer the developer. You are trying to see whether they understand tradeoffs, communicate clearly, and can work inside the scope you actually need.

CTA: Build your hiring packet

## Email 5: Review Before You Sign

Subject: Before you sign the quote

Preview: Vague assumptions become expensive later.

Body:

Before approving a developer quote or agency proposal, review the parts that usually create cost surprises:

- Undefined milestones
- Missing acceptance criteria
- Unclear ownership of auth, payments, hosting, or deployment
- Timeline assumptions
- Revision limits
- Post-launch support
- Handoff and documentation expectations

GhostCTO helps you turn a proposal into specific follow-up questions before money is committed.

It does not replace legal review of a contract, but it can help you identify technical and scope questions worth resolving before signing.

CTA: Review a quote

## Email 6: Keep The Workspace Current

Subject: Keep one source of technical context

Preview: Scope changes are easier when the context is organized.

Body:

Your first plan will change. That is normal.

Keep GhostCTO updated as you learn from customers, developers, advisors, and quotes. When the context changes, refresh the relevant materials:

- Scope brief
- Technical spec
- Stack recommendation
- Hiring packet
- Quote review notes

The benefit is not a perfect plan. The benefit is a clearer record of what changed, why it changed, and what needs to be clarified next.

CTA: Update your project context
